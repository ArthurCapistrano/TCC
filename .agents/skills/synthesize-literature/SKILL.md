---

# Field guidance

## ID

Use exactly the ID assigned during screening.

## Authors and Year

Use citation-friendly format.

Example:

Smith et al. (2024)

## Theme / Subtheme

Classify the main subject represented by the paper.

Prefer existing terminology from the research wiki when available.

Do not create unnecessary new categories.

## Study Type

Reuse the classification from evidence extraction.

## Method

Reuse the classification from evidence extraction.

## Context / Country

Reuse the contextual information extracted from the paper.

## Central Variables

Summarize the most important constructs or variables for comparison
with other studies.

## Relevant Findings

Summarize the findings most relevant to the current research question.

Target:

1-2 concise statements.

Do not attempt to summarize the entire paper.

## Research Gaps / Opportunities

Identify limitations or unresolved opportunities revealed by the study.

Distinguish:

AUTHOR-STATED GAP

from:

PROJECT-INTERPRETED OPPORTUNITY

Never present a project interpretation as an established research gap.

## Contribution to This Research

Explain specifically how this paper contributes to the current TCC.

Possible relationships include:

- theoretical foundation;
- methodology reference;
- similar problem;
- competing approach;
- supporting evidence;
- contradictory evidence;
- definition or taxonomy;
- identified limitation.

Avoid generic statements such as:

"This paper is relevant to the research."

---

# Rules

Do not hide contradictory findings.

Do not classify absence from the collected papers as proof of a literature gap.

Do not make every paper appear supportive of the project.

A paper may legitimately contribute by contradicting the current hypothesis.
